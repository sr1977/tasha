# Tasha — Impeccable Design Context

## Design Context

### Users
One household: Steve (and a partner in partner mode). Desktop browser,
full-screened, propped up in the training space. During workouts the screen is
read from 2–4 metres away, mid-exertion, often sweating — glanceability beats
density. Setup and pool management happen up close, before the session, in a
calmer state. Music (Spotify) and voice control are usually active; the screen
must work as a countdown clock first, an app second.

### Brand Personality
**Loud, physical, disciplined.** Drill-hall energy: a boxing-gym poster that
shouts at you, not a subscription fitness app that coaches gently. The work
interval should feel like a bell going off; the rest interval like catching
breath; the setup screens like taping your hands before the round — focused,
procedural, quiet.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme:** dark, always. Training happens against a dim room; the countdown
  owns the light in the frame.
- **Tone:** physical print, not digital gloss — heavy poster type, hard edges,
  ink-on-paper contrast. No glassmorphism, no neon glows, no gradient washes.
- **Color:** designer's choice. Interval color-coding (distinct hues per
  work/rest/round-rest/prep) is functional signal and must stay legible at a
  glance, but exact hues may change. Accent is rare and earned.
- **Anti-references:** none mandated by the user; by derivation avoid the
  corporate-fitness gloss and the sci-fi cockpit — both contradict "physical
  print".

### Design Principles
1. **The clock is the hero.** Every workout-screen decision defers to countdown
   legibility at 3 metres. Nothing may compete with it.
2. **Shout when it matters, shut up when it doesn't.** Work intervals are loud
   and total; setup/pool screens are quiet, procedural, disciplined.
3. **Physical, not digital.** Poster type, hard edges, honest surfaces. If an
   effect couldn't exist on printed paper or painted gym signage, question it.
4. **Color is state, not decoration.** Interval kind drives the palette;
   decorative accents are rare and earn their place.
5. **Motion is a bell, not wallpaper.** Interval transitions may punch; nothing
   else moves. Respect prefers-reduced-motion.
